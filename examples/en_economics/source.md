# Minimum wages and youth employment

A large literature examines whether minimum wage increases reduce employment. Card and Krueger (1994) compare fast-food restaurants in New Jersey and Pennsylvania and find no evidence of job losses after the 1992 increase. This paper revisits the question with a panel of 312 counties observed between 2010 and 2019. The difference-in-differences estimate is 0.014, with a standard error of 0.009: employment of workers aged 16 to 19 in treated counties is 1.4 percentage points higher than in comparison counties, but the coefficient is not statistically significant at the 10% level. Pre-treatment coefficients are close to zero, which is consistent with parallel trends, although the test has low power. The policy raised the annual wage bill by about 2.4 billion dollars.
